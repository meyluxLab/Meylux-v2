It authorizes no implementation.

## 2. Sole architectural question

Determine, from first principles and current evidence:

> **Given the actual limitations established for MEXC public candle finality, trade sequencing, completeness, REST reconciliation, and related provider semantics, is there any technically defensible architecture or semantic strategy by which Meylux can retain substantially more of MEXC's legitimate market-data capabilities without introducing unsupported provider facts into the canonical model?**

The objective is:

**MAXIMUM VALID MEXC CAPABILITY**

subject to:

**NO UNSUPPORTED CANONICAL FACTS**

The investigation MUST NOT assume that a solution exists and MUST NOT force MEXC observations into the existing canonical candle model merely to preserve functionality.

## 3. Mandatory semantic distinction

The assessment MUST distinguish explicitly between:

1. **A particular mechanism fails** — e.g. the `tradeId` sequence-integrity mechanism investigated by `TO-P2-011`.
2. **A particular architectural route fails** — e.g. a Trade→Candle completeness architecture whose proof depends on that unsupported mechanism.
3. **No defensible alternative route has currently been identified** — a broader conclusion that requires materially broader evidence and must not be inferred merely from failure of one mechanism.

Failure of one mechanism does not prove that all MEXC capabilities are unusable.

Conversely, theoretical possibility of another mechanism does not establish that it is defensible.

## 4. First-principles investigation

Do not assume that the solution must rely on:

- provider `is_closed`;
- `tradeId` continuity;
- `windowEnd`;
- elapsed time;
- receipt time;
- local clock;
- repeated observation;
- message ordering;
- OHLC temporal geometry;
- REST availability;
- grace periods;
- synthetic finality flags;
- heuristic closure;
- or any previously rejected semantic inference.

Independently investigate whether another provider-grounded mechanism exists. If none exists, establish that negative result rigorously.

## 5. Required architectural directions

These are investigation prompts, not prescribed solutions.

### 5.1 Provider evidence versus canonical finality

Determine whether more MEXC information can be preserved as provider evidence without asserting finalized canonical candle semantics.

Inspect whether an already-existing raw, acquisition, staging, evidence, provisional or other architectural boundary can legitimately preserve useful information.

Do not invent a new lifecycle state or contract merely to make the provider fit.

If an architecture or contract extension is genuinely required, stop at the design/decision boundary and identify the exact governance/change-control action required.

### 5.2 Alternative completeness or closure mechanisms

Investigate whether MEXC exposes any authoritative mechanism not previously assessed, including where genuinely relevant:

- documented event semantics;
- provider sequencing guarantees other than `tradeId`;
- snapshot/update relationships;
- synchronization markers;
- subscription semantics;
- endpoint relationships;
- provider timestamps with explicitly documented meaning;
- state-transition guarantees;
- other official provider mechanisms.

No such mechanism may be assumed to exist.

### 5.3 Multiple official evidence sources

Determine whether multiple official MEXC evidence sources can establish a stronger property when combined, including WebSocket, REST, timestamps, snapshots, or other official market-data endpoints.

Distinguish a provider-grounded guarantee from mere correlation, repeated observation, statistical agreement, or temporal coincidence.

### 5.4 Capability-specific semantics

Analyze MEXC capabilities individually rather than generalizing from candle finality or trade completeness.

At minimum consider whether legitimate information can be retained or consumed as:

- raw trade evidence;
- provider observations;
- market-state observations;
- non-final interval information;
- historical observations;
- analytical features whose semantic requirements differ from finalized canonical candles;
- other public market data whose required guarantees differ.

Do not declare unrelated capabilities unusable without evidence.

### 5.5 Canonicalization boundary

Determine whether the current canonicalization boundary can remain fully truthful while preserving more MEXC information upstream.

The question is NOT whether `CanonicalCandle.is_closed` can be weakened.

If preserving additional semantics requires a contract or architecture change, identify exactly:

- what must change;
- why the current architecture cannot express the required semantics;
- which invariants would be affected;
- and what governance/change-control action would be required.

Do not implement the change.

### 5.6 Historical versus live populations

Analyze separately:

- historical MEXC data;
- live WebSocket data;
- REST-observed data;
- WebSocket-observed data;
- independently reconciled data.

Do not generalize a mechanism valid for one population to another without evidence.

## 6. Semantic-laundering prohibition

For every candidate mechanism answer:

> **Does the mechanism establish the required fact, or merely rename an inference as a fact?**

Explicitly reject mechanisms based on:

- time-derived finality;
- next-interval observation treated as closure proof;
- repeated identical observations;
- REST availability treated as closure;
- statistical agreement treated as completeness;
- absence of further messages treated as completion;
- internally invented states presented as provider facts;
- any provider-specific inference hidden behind a generic canonical field.

## 8. Required candidate-mechanism analysis

For every materially plausible mechanism, report:

- required provider guarantee;
- authoritative documentation;
- live observation evidence, if available;
- repository facts;
- whether the guarantee is actually established;
- completeness implications;
- finality implications;

- identity implications;
- replay implications;
- historical/live applicability;
- failure modes;
- compatibility with current contracts;
- whether formal change control would be required;
- whether the mechanism is merely inferential and therefore unsuitable for canonical truth.

A finite observation cannot establish a universal provider guarantee unless the provider contract itself supplies the guarantee.
